# Outreach compliance rule sheet: ~25 Austin subcontractor calls and emails

**Not legal advice.** A researcher's summary, checked 2026-11-13, for a solo founder who will hand-dial and email
about 25 Austin electricians, plumbers and HVAC firms found in City of Austin permit records.
Confidence tags: **[P]** primary source read for this sheet · **[S]** secondary (law-firm or news) · **[G]** general understanding, not checked against a source this session.

## 2. FTC Telemarketing Sales Rule (16 CFR 310)

- **The B2B exemption (310.6(b)(7)) covers calls to a business to induce a purchase. It has limits.** Since the 2024 amendments, B2B calls must still follow **310.3(a)(2)** (no material misrepresentations, for example about cost, what the product does, or affiliations) and **310.3(a)(4)** (no false or misleading statements to induce payment). [P: eCFR 310.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-310/section-310.6); [P: 2024 final rule, Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/04/16/2024-07180/telemarketing-sales-rule)
- The expanded recordkeeping rules in 310.5 do **not** apply to B2B sellers. [S: Husch Blackwell](https://huschblackwell.com/newsandinsights/ftc-amends-telemarketing-sales-rule-to-increase-recordkeeping-requirements-and-cover-business-to-business-telemarketing-calls)
- In practice: no misrepresentation on calls. Don't say the city endorses you, don't invent customers or results, and say plainly that the pilot is free and that a paid product may follow. A sole proprietor reached on a personal cell may not count as a "business" for the exemption [G], so the honesty rules are the minimum.

## 3. Texas

- **Calling hours (Bus. & Com. Code 301.051).** Mon–Sat 9 a.m.–9 p.m., Sunday noon–9 p.m. These attach to "consumer telephone calls" to a *residential* number about a *consumer* good or service, so B2B is arguably outside them. Follow them anyway. Exceptions include calls the person expressly asked for and prior business relationships. The penalty is up to $10,000 per knowing violation. [P: BC ch. 301](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.301.htm)
- **Texas no-call list (ch. 304).** It covers "telemarketing calls", meaning calls to sell a *consumer* good or service (personal, family or household use). 304.004 also exempts non-fax calls to a business unless the business has said it doesn't want them. A B2B data product is not a consumer good, so ch. 304 is unlikely to apply. Note that the Texas list includes Texas numbers on the National DNC. **Caller ID (304.151):** don't block caller ID or send misleading caller ID. [P: BC ch. 304](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.304.htm)
- **Telephone solicitation registration (ch. 302). This is the main Texas risk.**
  - "Telephone solicitation" is any call or text by a seller to induce a person to buy, rent, **claim or receive** an item, and "item" includes a service. The definition has no consumer-only limit. A free pilot could count as "receiving" a service. [P: BC ch. 302](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.302.htm)
  - Registration means one certificate per location, filed with the Secretary of State, with a $10,000 security deposit. [P / S](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/texas-mini-tcpa-goes-into-effect-september-1-2025-what-you-need-to-know-privacy)
  - Possible exemptions:
    - **302.059(1)**: the call doesn't try to complete a sale, makes no major sales presentation by phone, and arranges any face-to-face presentation for later.
    - **302.061**: an isolated transaction. 25 similar calls likely don't qualify.
    - **302.056** (business buyers who resell or manufacture) does **not** fit.
    - There is **no general B2B exemption** in ch. 302.
  - An exemption must be proven by the person who claims it (302.051).
  - **SB 140 (2025, eff. Sept 1, 2025)** amended 302.001 and 302.003. It brought texts in explicitly and made violations actionable under the DTPA (302.303), which opens private suits. [P: amendment notes in BC ch. 302](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.302.htm); [S: Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/texas-mini-tcpa-goes-into-effect-september-1-2025-what-you-need-to-know-privacy)
  - Correction to the brief: the change was **SB 140 (2025)**. I could not confirm that "SB 1102 (2023)" amended ch. 302.
  - Penalties are up to $5,000 per violation (AG civil penalty), and knowing violations are Class A misdemeanors. Confirm the exact SB 140 damages with counsel.
  - **Mitigation:** keep calls to discovery questions plus "may I email you details or set a meeting?" Close nothing on the phone and take no payment.

## 4. CAN-SPAM (email)

- It applies to B2B email ("no exception for business-to-business email"). A **commercial** message (one whose primary purpose is to promote a product or service) must have:
  - accurate From, To and routing headers
  - a subject line that isn't deceptive
  - a clear disclosure that it is an advertisement
  - a valid **physical postal address** (a street address, a registered P.O. box or a registered private mailbox)
  - a clear opt-out that keeps working for **30 days** after sending and is honored within **10 business days**
  - Penalty: up to $53,088 per email (2024 figure). [P: FTC compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- **A pure research or interview request** with no promotion probably falls in the "other" category, which is not commercial, so only the header-truthfulness rule applies. **Once the email mentions the pilot or the product, treat it as commercial.** Under the mixed-message test, the subject line and the placement of commercial content decide [P, same guide]. Simplest path: put the full CAN-SPAM footer on every email.

## 6. Founder checklist

**Do**
- Before calling, check each number: is it clearly a business line (shown on the company website or Google listing, or a landline)?
  - If it is a **cell number, or you can't tell**, scrub it against the National DNC. Up to 5 area codes are free; 512 and 737 cover Austin [G: [telemarketing.donotcall.gov](https://telemarketing.donotcall.gov/)].
  - If the number is registered, **email first** and call only after they invite you.
- Call from your real number with caller ID on, Mon–Sat 9 a.m.–9 p.m. and Sun noon–9 p.m. Central.
- Open every call with: "This is [name] from [company]. I'm researching how trade contractors find work. Do you have 5 minutes?" Leave a callback number.
- Ask questions first. Mention the pilot only as "free, 4 weeks, and a paid version may follow." Don't close anything on the call.
- Log every "don't call / don't email" right away, honor it across all channels for good, and confirm it if asked.
- Emails: one-to-one, accurate From and subject, plus a footer with your postal address and "Reply 'stop' and I won't email again."
- Keep a one-paragraph written DNC policy (who, what, 10 business days) and this log.

**Don't**
- Use dialers, auto-texts, voicemail drops, recorded messages, spoofed caller ID or \*67.
- Cold-text anyone. Text only after they text you first, and stop on any "stop".
- Call any number more than twice without a reply, or a second time within 12 months if it is DNC-registered or the person said no.
- Claim a city affiliation or endorsement, invent customers or results, or hide that the product will be paid.
- Contact homeowners or owner-individuals from the permits, or use owner names.
- Take payment or sign anything on the phone (keeps the ch. 302.059 exemption available).

**Suppression log** (CSV, append-only, keep 5 years)
