# Austin Sub Outreach + Interview Kit (pilot validation)

**Goal of every call:** learn *when this sub usually gets hired* compared with the building permit (BP), and *how they win new builders/GCs*. Selling the pilot comes second. A "no" that tells you when they get hired still counts as a good result.

**Two pitches are being tested, not one.**
- **Pitch A, early leads:** a weekly list of jobs where the BP is issued and no trade permit exists yet.
- **Pitch B, GC relationship intelligence:** which builders/GCs use which subs (including competitors), which GCs are new or small and have no regular sub yet, and when a GC switches subs.

Why B exists: the timing analysis found that when a GC has prior work in a trade, about 94% of its trade permits go to a sub it already used (about 98% for big GCs). At most about 25% of trade jobs look open to a new sub. If that holds, most "early leads" are already spoken for, and the real opening is the relationship with the GC. Section 3, part A2, tests B without pitching it.

**Ground rules (keep them):**
- 1:1 only. Manual dial from your own phone, and each email written to one person. No autodialer, no prerecorded messages, no texts unless they text you first.
- Use only the business numbers and emails printed on the sub's **own** public permits. Never use homeowner names or homeowner contacts (CLAUDE.md rule 5).
- Every channel gets honest wording, a real name, and a way to opt out. Log every opt-out on the spot and never contact that company again.
- Email: at most 2 touches. Phone: at most 2 attempts plus 1 voicemail. Then stop.

## 1. Email

### Subject lines (pick one; all honest, no "Re:" and no fake urgency)
1. `Question about your {{trade}} permit on {{recent_permit_address_street_only}}`
2. `Austin {{trade}} subs: how do you win new builders?`
3. `Quick question from a local founder (not a sales pitch)`

### Cold email (about 125 words; covers both pitches, leads with builder relationships)
> Hi {{first_name_or_"there"}},
>
> I'm {{founder_name}}. I'm building a small tool in Austin, and I found {{company}} through public City of Austin permit records, including your {{trade}} permit on {{recent_permit_address_street_only}}.
>
> The permit data shows which builders work with which subs, which builders are new in town, and when a builder switches subs. It also shows new jobs weeks before the trade permits get pulled. I'm trying to learn which of those, if any, would actually help you win work.
>
> Could I ask you a few questions on a 10-minute call? I'm not selling anything. If something turns out to be useful, I'd offer a free 4-week trial.
>
> Reply with a good time, or call me at {{founder_phone}}.
>
> {{founder_name}}
> {{founder_postal_address}}
> *Don't want to hear from me? Reply "no" and I won't contact you again.*

### Follow-up (one only, 4–5 business days later, same thread, about 45 words)
> Hi {{first_name_or_"there"}}, just bumping this once. I'm trying to learn how {{trade}} subs in Austin win new builders and when they get hired on a job. Got 10 minutes this week? If not, no problem. I won't follow up again.
>
> {{founder_name}} · {{founder_phone}} · {{founder_postal_address}}
> *Reply "no" to be removed.*

**CAN-SPAM checklist:** accurate From name, subject that matches the body, a valid postal address (a PO box or registered mailbox is fine), an opt-out that works, and opt-outs honored within 10 business days (do it the same day). These rules cover B2B email too.

## 2. Phone script

### Opener (about 45 seconds)
> "Hi, is this {{company}}? ... My name's {{founder_name}}. I'm a local founder here in Austin. I got this number off your own permit with the City, the {{trade}} job on {{street}}. This isn't a sales call. I'm researching how {{trade}} subs win new builders and get their work, and I'd like to ask you three or four quick questions. **Got 2 minutes now, or is there a better time to call back?**"

- **Yes:** go to the interview (section 3). Keep to the time they gave you.
- **Better time:** "What day and time works?" Write it down, call exactly then, and say so: "You told me Thursday at 4."

### Gatekeeper / office manager
> "Hi, I'm {{founder_name}}, a local founder. I'm not selling anything. I'm asking {{trade}} contractors a couple of questions about how they find jobs. Who handles bidding new work there? ... When's a good time to catch them?"
- If they say "I handle that": **interview them.** Office managers often know the timing better than the owner does.
- If they say "send an email": get the address, send the cold email that same day, and put "Spoke with {{name}} in your office" on the first line.

### Voicemail (about 15 seconds, leave only one)
> "Hi, this is {{founder_name}}, a local founder in Austin. I got your number from your {{trade}} permit on {{street}}. I'm not selling anything. I have a couple of quick questions about how you get hired on jobs. My number is {{founder_phone}}, again {{founder_phone}}. Thanks."

### Objections
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Not interested." | "Totally fair. One question before I go, if that's OK: on a job like {{street}}, are you usually hired before the builder pulls their permit or after?" If they still say no: "Thanks, I'll leave you alone." |
| "We have enough work." | "Good to hear. That's actually what I'm trying to learn. Are you turning jobs down right now?" (Log it as `at_capacity`. It still counts as a useful data point.) |
| "How'd you get my number?" | "It's on your permit for {{street}}. The City of Austin publishes permits publicly. I only use the business number that's on the permit." |
| "Is this a sales call?" | "No. Right now I'm only asking questions. Later I may offer a free 4-week trial, and you can say no. Nothing to buy." |
| "Send me an email." | "Sure, what's the best address?" Then: "And just so I put the right question in it: are you usually brought in before or after the builder's permit?" Send it the same day. |
| "Who are you with?" | "Just me. I'm building this myself in Austin. No company behind it yet." |

### Do-not-call (do this right away, with no pushback)
> "Understood. I'll take you off my list right now. Sorry to bother you."
Mark `do_not_contact=yes` before you dial the next number. Never call, email, or add that company to the pilot again.

## 3. 12-minute discovery interview (Mom Test)

**Rules:** ask about **what happened**, not what they *would* do. Talk about their life, not your idea. Don't pitch until the last two minutes. If they say "I'd totally use that," treat it as noise. Ask "When did that last happen?"

**Before you dial:** pull one of their recent trade permits: street name, trade permit issue date, the matching building permit date, and the builder on the BP. Say only the street, never the homeowner.

### A. The key question, about their own job (about 4 minutes)
1. "Your {{trade}} permit on **{{street}}**, issued around **{{trade_permit_date}}**. Do you remember that job?"
2. "**When did you first hear about it?** About how long before you pulled your permit?"
3. "**Who brought you in?** The builder, a GC, the homeowner, a repeat customer, someone else?"
4. "The builder's permit was issued **{{bp_date}}**. Were you already lined up by then, or did you get the call after?"
5. "Is that normal for you, or was that job different? Think of the last 3 jobs. Same timing?"

Write down the actual answer in days or weeks if you can, e.g. "hired about 3 weeks before BP" or "called 2 weeks after BP."

### A2. How they win builders/GCs (about 3 minutes; tests pitch B, the lead hypothesis)
11. "Think of the **last builder or GC you started working with**. How did that start? Who called whom, and when?"
12. "How many builders/GCs give you most of your work? Has that list changed in the last year? Did you **lose one to another sub**? How did you find out?"
13. "When a **new builder** shows up in your area, how do you hear about them today? How long after they start pulling permits?"
14. "Do you know **which subs your competitors work for**, or which builders use them? How do you find out?" (Listen for whether they care.)
15. "Do you do anything today to go after builders: visits, lunches, a sales person, bid lists? **What did that cost and what did it produce last time?**"

Don't describe pitch B. If they ask what you'd build, say "a few options; I'm trying to learn which one matters," and move on.

### B. Where their work comes from today (about 2 minutes)
6. "Out of your last 10 jobs, roughly how many came from: the same few builders/GCs, repeat or referral, Angi/Thumbtack/HomeAdvisor/Google, supply house or other subs, or bid sites?"
7. "**How many jobs did you bid last month, and how many did you win?**"

### C. Capacity and pain (about 2 minutes)
8. "Right now, would you take on more work, or are you turning jobs down?"
9. "**What do you pay for leads today?** Angi, ads, a bid service, anything? About how much a month?" (Find out what they spend. Don't ask what they'd pay.)
10. "Last time you went looking for new work, what did you actually do?"

### D. Close (about 2 minutes). See section 4.

### What to listen for
| Commitment (real signal) | Compliment / noise (ignore it) |
|---|---|
| Gives a date for the weekly call | "Sounds cool" |
| Gives you an email address for the list | "I'd probably use that" |
| Already pays for leads, and names the dollar amount | "Everyone needs more work" |
| Forwards you to their estimator | "Send me some info" (with no date) |
| Tells you a specific job they lost because they heard too late | Generic complaints about the industry |

### Scoring each interview (fill in `hired_timing`)
- **Hired-early:** usually lined up **before or when** the BP is issued (steady builder relationships). *Our signal does little for them.* Note which builder. The builder might be the real customer.
- **Hired-in-gap:** usually brought in **after** the BP, or bids against others weeks after it. *Our signal is valuable.* This is the pilot target.
- **Mixed:** depends on the job type, e.g. new homes early but remodels and additions in the gap. Note **which permit types** fall in the gap. That shapes what goes on the list.
- **Unknown:** they couldn't or wouldn't say. Don't guess. Log it as unknown.

Also note: at capacity (yes/no), money spent on leads today, and the main source of work.

Score pitch B separately in `gc_interest`:
- **strong:** recently tried to win a new builder, or lost one to a competitor, and spent time or money on it (named an amount or an action).
- **weak:** "would be nice", no recent action.
- **none:** happy with current builders, not looking.

A sub can be Hired-early (pitch A useless) and still strong on pitch B. That combination is the most important thing to notice, because the timing data predicts it will be common.

## 4. Pilot close

Offer pitch A if they're Hired-in-gap or Mixed **and** open to more work. If they're Hired-early but `gc_interest` is strong, offer the pitch B variant below instead. Don't offer both on one call.

> "Here's what I'd like to try. For 4 weeks, free, I'll email you one short list every Monday: 10 to 20 Austin jobs where the builder's permit was just issued and no {{trade}} permit has been pulled yet. Each job comes with the address, the builder, the permit type, and the date. In return, I'd ask for a 15-minute call each week where you tell me which ones were new to you and early enough to bid. Want to try it?"

**Pitch B variant (Hired-early, strong GC interest):**
> "Different idea, then. For 4 weeks, free, every Monday I'd send you a short list of builders who are new or small in your area and pulled permits this month, plus any builder whose {{trade}} work moved to a different sub. Same deal: 15 minutes a week telling me which ones you didn't know about and whether you reached out. Want to try it?"

Never put a competitor's name in writing to the sub until counsel has looked at it (see `outreach-compliance.md`, open questions). Builder company names from permits are fine.

**What "yes" commits them to (say it out loud and confirm it in a 1:1 email):**
- One email address for the list.
- A **15-minute call each week for 4 weeks** at a time they pick. Put it on the calendar now.
- An honest answer for each lead: *new to me? early enough to bid? did I contact anyone?*
- Nothing to buy, no contract, and they can stop any week.

**What you commit to:** the list arrives on time, every job is a real permit with the date shown, there are no homeowner names, and you stop when they say stop.

**Launch gate:** at least 3 of 5 pilot subs say the leads were "new to me and early enough to bid" (pitch A), or "new to me, and I contacted the builder" (pitch B). Report the two pitches separately; don't add them together.
**Push back on fake yeses:** if they won't book the first call, it's not a yes. Log it as `maybe`.

## 5. Tracking sheet (CSV header)

```csv
date,company,trade,phone,email,source_permit_id,street,attempt_no,channel,outcome,contact_role,hired_timing,days_hired_vs_bp,gc_count,lost_gc_last_year,gc_interest,source_of_work,bids_last_month,wins_last_month,at_capacity,pays_for_leads,lead_spend_month_usd,interest_pilot,follow_up,follow_up_date,do_not_contact,notes
```
Allowed values:
- `channel`: call | voicemail | email | email_followup | inbound
- `outcome`: connected | gatekeeper | voicemail | no_answer | wrong_number | callback_set | declined | interviewed
- `hired_timing`: hired_early | hired_in_gap | mixed | unknown
- `days_hired_vs_bp`: a signed number of days (negative = hired before the BP). Leave blank if unknown.
- `source_of_work`: builder_gc | repeat_referral | marketplace | supply_house | bid_site | other. Use `;` to list more than one.
- `gc_interest`: strong | weak | none | unknown
- `interest_pilot`: yes_booked_a | yes_booked_b | maybe | no | not_offered
- `do_not_contact`: yes | no. Once it's `yes`, it never changes back.

Keep this sheet off any shared drive. It's a call log, not a product data source.

## 6. How to run the week

**Targets: 25 companies.** Mix about 10 electrical, 8 plumbing, and 7 HVAC. Use `austin-targets.csv`, which is weighted toward subs that work for many different and small or new builders (those are the subs who still compete for new builders). Big shops tied to one or two production builders are usually hired early. Pull one recent permit per target before you dial.

**When to call: Tue–Thu.** Avoid Monday (scheduling chaos) and Friday (wrapping up the week).
- **9:00–10:30 am:** the office manager is in and the owner may be on the phone between job sites.
- **4:00–6:00 pm:** crews are back, and owners are doing estimates and paperwork.
- **Not 7:00 am.** It's the trades' natural hour, but federal rules (47 CFR 64.1200) bar sales-type calls before 8 am. Texas Bus. & Com. Code §301.051 bars telephone solicitation before 9 am on weekdays, and on Sunday before noon. A research call arguably isn't a "solicitation," but the pilot offer gets close, so stay inside 9 am–6 pm. *(Add this to `docs/COMPLIANCE.md` as an open question. Also ask a lawyer whether Texas telemarketing registration, Bus. & Com. Code ch. 302, applies to a free offer. Free offers are likely exempt, but this hasn't been confirmed.)*
- Many sole-proprietor subs list a cell phone. Manual 1:1 calls are fine. Never use an autodialer or prerecorded message to a cell.

**Cadence:**
- **Tue:** email all 25 in the morning. Call 12 of them 4–6 pm.
- **Wed:** call the other 13 from 9 to 10:30. Second attempt to Tuesday's no-answers 4–6 pm (leave a voicemail on this attempt).
- **Thu:** keep callbacks and interviews. Second attempt to Wednesday's no-answers.
- **Following Mon/Tue:** send the one follow-up email to anyone who hasn't replied and hasn't opted out. Then stop with that list.

**Expected results (realistic, not promised):** cold B2B email reply rates are commonly reported at about 1–5%. Cold-call connect rates are commonly reported in the single digits to low teens per dial. A permit-specific, local, non-sales opener should do better than average, but plan low. Out of 25 targets, expect:
- 1–2 email replies
- 5–8 live conversations across two call attempts
- 3–5 completed interviews
- 0–2 pilot yeses

**You'll likely need 2–3 weeks (50–75 targets) to sign 5 pilot subs.** These are planning numbers from general cold-outreach benchmarks, not Austin data. Replace them with your own numbers after week 1.

**Stop rules:**
- **Per contact:** 2 emails, 2 call attempts, 1 voicemail, then stop. Any "no," "stop," or "remove" means stop right away and mark it DNC.
- **Per week:** stop dialing once you have 5 completed interviews. Analyze them before you do more.
- **Pitch A kill:** if **at least 7 of the first 10 interviews are Hired-early**, stop offering pitch A. Given the 94% repeat-sub finding, this is the most likely outcome; plan for it.
- **Pitch A go:** if **at least 4 of 10 are Hired-in-gap or Mixed and open to more work**, offer pitch A and work toward the 3-of-5 gate.
- **Pitch B go:** if **at least 4 of 10 are `gc_interest=strong`**, offer pitch B to those subs.
- **Both dead:** if neither go rule fires after 10 interviews, stop outreach and write up what they *did* say they spend time and money on. Don't keep dialing hoping the numbers change.
- **Channel check:** if 25 targets produce fewer than 2 conversations, fix the list (wrong numbers? mostly big firms?) before you dial more.
- **Complaint:** if anyone complains about how they were contacted, stop for the day and review this script.
