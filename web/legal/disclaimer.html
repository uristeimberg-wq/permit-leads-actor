Giboro shows public records as the agencies publish them. Read this before relying on what you see.

Not an official source

Giboro is not affiliated with or endorsed by any city, county or state agency. The issuing agency's own record is the official one. Check it before you bid, sign or decide anything.

Records arrive late

Agencies publish permits after a delay (Austin about 2 to 3 days after issue; Miami-Dade weekly), and we read each source on a schedule. A permit filed recently may not be here yet. The "Updated" badge in the app shows when we last read each city.

Values are the applicant's estimate

The value on a permit is what the applicant declared when filing, not a contract price or the final cost. Some agencies copy a whole project's total onto each of its permits; we mark those "on N permits". Values under $100 or of $1 billion and more are placeholders or typos at the source, so we don't show them.

Statuses and categories are the agency's

We group each agency's own status wording into a few stages, and the residential or non-residential label is the agency's own. A status can change at the agency before we read it again.

Locations can be approximate

Where we don't hold a point for an address, the map uses the centre of its ZIP code or city, and says so.

Contacts are not checked

Phone numbers and company names are as written on the permit. Nobody has called to check them. Giboro never contacts anyone on your behalf.

Photos may be old

Street View photos come from Google and can be older than the permit, or show a nearby spot rather than the job site. Each photo shows its capture date and distance.

Not professional advice

Nothing on Giboro is legal, financial, engineering or code-compliance advice.
